# Planning the night and choosing targets

A little planning turns a clear night into good data. The main questions are: what is
well placed tonight, does it fit my field of view, how long can I follow it, and does
the Moon or weather get in the way.

## Altitude and the meridian

A target is best imaged when it is highest in the sky, because you look through the
least atmosphere (lowest airmass), so seeing is steadier and extinction is lowest.
That happens as it crosses the meridian (transits). Try to image within a few hours
of transit, and generally stay above about 30 degrees altitude; below that, stars
bloat and detail smears. Plan the session so your target climbs through or sits near
its highest point during your imaging window.

## Meridian flip

On a German equatorial mount, a target crossing the meridian forces the mount to
swap sides so the scope does not hit the tripod. This "meridian flip" flips the image
top-to-bottom and needs a re-center (plate solve) and often a refocus and guide
recalibration. Plan for it: either start the target before the meridian and let it
flip mid-session, or begin after the flip. Automated flips handle the slew, re-center,
and resume; just leave settling time.

## Field of view and framing

Match the target's angular size to your field of view. Big objects (Andromeda, the
North America Nebula, the Veil) suit short focal lengths and wide fields or a mosaic;
small galaxies and planetary nebulae suit long focal lengths. Plate solving plus a
framing/mosaic tool lets you preview the exact framing and rotation before you commit
the night to it. Rotating the camera to fit an elongated target better is worth the
few minutes.

## Seasons and what to shoot

- Winter (northern): Orion (M42), the Horsehead and Flame, the Rosette, M45 Pleiades.
- Spring: galaxy season, Leo Triplet, M81/M82, the Whirlpool M51, the Markarian Chain.
- Summer: the Milky Way core region, Lagoon and Trifid, the Veil, the North America
  and Pelican, Elephant's Trunk (great in narrowband).
- Autumn: Andromeda M31, the Triangulum M33, the Heart and Soul, the Pacman.

Match the target type to conditions: emission nebulae in narrowband work under Moon
and light pollution; galaxies and reflection nebulae want dark, moonless skies.

## Weather and seeing

Clouds end a session, but transparency (haze) and seeing (atmospheric steadiness)
also matter. Poor seeing bloats stars and limits resolution, so save fine, small
targets for steady nights and shoot wide nebulae when seeing is soft. Check a
forecast, watch the humidity and dew point (run a dew heater when they converge),
and have a backup target lower or higher in the sky in case clouds roll through part
of the sky.
